## Essence
People "hire" products to make progress in a circumstance — the job, not the demographic, is the unit of demand. Switching happens when (push of the current struggle + pull of the new solution) exceeds (anxiety of the new + habit of the present). Demand claims that don't identify the job and the four forces are speculation.

## Interrogation set
1. What is the job statement — "when [circumstance], help me [progress], so that [outcome]"? Can the draft fill it without naming the product?
2. What are buyers using TODAY for this job (including spreadsheets, interns, and doing nothing)? That incumbent — not the named competitor — is the real rival.
3. What is the push/pull vs anxiety/habit balance? Which force does the draft ignore — usually switching anxiety?
4. Is the biggest opportunity current spenders or NON-consumers priced/skilled out of the market? Which does the draft size?
5. What evidence shows the struggle is painful enough to pay for — support tickets, workarounds, current spend — vs. "users want"?
6. Functional job aside: what emotional/social job matters here (looking competent to a boss, reducing blame risk)?

## How to apply
- Reject segments defined by firmographics alone; demand at least one circumstance-based segment.
- Weak answer smell: "SMBs need efficiency"; feature lists as demand proof; TAM counted while the job is done free today.
- Does NOT apply: pure supply-side/competitor-landscape questions.

## Output contract
Revision must state the job, the today-alternative, the four switching forces with evidence, and re-cut at least one segment by circumstance rather than demographics.
