## Essence
Power = a benefit (margin or share advantage) PLUS a barrier (why competitors can't or won't arbitrage it away). Exactly seven exist: Scale Economies, Network Economies, Counter-Positioning, Switching Costs, Branding, Cornered Resource, Process Power. Anything else — features, speed, "AI" — is operational excellence: valuable, copyable, not power.

## Interrogation set
1. Which of the seven powers, BY NAME, does the claimed advantage map to? If none, say plainly: no moat, only a head start.
2. For the claimed power, state the benefit AND the barrier separately. A benefit without a barrier is an invitation.
3. Network effects claimed: does user N+1 actually make the product better for user N, or is it just a big user count?
4. Switching costs claimed: what would it genuinely cost a customer to leave (data, integration, retraining, risk) — in numbers?
5. Counter-positioning: why would the incumbent NOT copy this — what in their P&L or channel makes copying self-harming?
6. Which power could this business plausibly BUILD in 24 months, and what would the draft's recommendation need to change to build it?

## How to apply
- Power claims must survive the "well-funded fast-follower" test at 12 and 36 months.
- Weak answer smell: "our AI is better" as moat; "brand" claimed by a company nobody knows; three powers claimed at once with evidence for none.
- Does NOT apply: commodity-play recommendations that explicitly compete on cost/execution.

## Output contract
Revision must name the power (or admit none exists), separate benefit from barrier with evidence, and state the 24-month power-building path if currently powerless.
