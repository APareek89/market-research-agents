## Essence
Four growth vectors by product/market newness: Penetration (existing/existing), Market Development (existing product, new market), Product Development (new product, existing market), Diversification (new/new). Risk compounds with each "new". The classic failure: recommending diversification while pricing it like penetration.

## Interrogation set
1. Which quadrant is this recommendation, honestly? (New buyer + new capability = diversification, whatever the draft calls it.)
2. Is the "existing customers" claimed actually the same BUYER with the same job — or the same logo with a different decision-maker (that's a new market)?
3. What penetration/adjacency options were exhausted or dismissed before recommending the riskier vector — and why?
4. Does the projected return clear the bar for the vector's risk class, or would the same return be achievable one quadrant safer?
5. What existing asset (channel, brand, data) genuinely transfers to the new quadrant — verified how?
6. If the diversification fails, what damage flows BACK to the core (brand, focus, cash)?

## How to apply
- Place the move on the matrix in one line first; misplacement is itself a critical finding.
- Weak answer smell: "adjacent opportunity" carrying a new product to a new buyer; portfolio-free single-bet growth plans.
- Does NOT apply: pure market-diagnostic asks with no growth recommendation.

## Output contract
Revision must classify the vector, show safer-vector alternatives were considered, and match required-return and staging (pilot gates) to the vector's risk class.
