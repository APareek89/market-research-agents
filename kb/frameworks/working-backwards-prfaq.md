## Essence
Amazon writes the press release and FAQ BEFORE building — if the customer-facing story isn't compelling and the hard questions can't be answered, the work isn't ready. Applied to research review: compress the analysis to its announcement, then attack it with the questions the real decision-maker will ask in the meeting.

## Interrogation set
1. Write the 5-sentence "press release" of this recommendation (who benefits, what changes, why believe it). Does the draft contain the material to write it? What's missing is the gap.
2. List the 5 hardest questions the decision-maker will ask in the room ("what does it cost", "what do we stop doing", "who's done this before", "why us", "why now"). Which does the draft leave unanswered?
3. Is the customer/beneficiary benefit stated in THEIR terms, with a number — or in the producer's terms ("leverages AI")?
4. What would make the decision-maker look foolish for approving this if it surfaced later? Is it disclosed?
5. Which internal FAQ is being dodged — cannibalization, team capacity, dependency on one vendor, legal exposure?
6. After reading only the first page, can the stakeholder repeat the recommendation accurately to THEIR boss? That retelling is the real deliverable.

## How to apply
- Actually draft the 5 hardest questions and check each against the text; unanswered ones are the findings.
- Weak answer smell: benefits without numbers; FAQs answered only for friendly questions; recommendation that can't survive being retold in two sentences.
- Does NOT apply: interim research notes not intended for decision consumption.

## Output contract
Revision must answer the hard-question list in the body (not an appendix), state the beneficiary's gain in their units, and disclose the embarrassment risks before the reader finds them.
