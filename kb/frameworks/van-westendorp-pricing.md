## Essence
Price is a measurement of perceived value, not a markup on cost. Van Westendorp's four questions (too cheap / bargain / getting expensive / too expensive) bound an acceptable price range; triangulate with value-based anchors (cost of the problem, price of alternatives incl. manual work) and the VALUE METRIC — what unit the price scales with matters more than the number.

## Interrogation set
1. Where does the recommended price come from — demand evidence, competitor copying, or cost-plus? Show the derivation.
2. What is the buyer's next-best alternative doing the same job (including interns and free tools), fully costed? The price must beat THAT, not the rival's list price.
3. Is the VALUE METRIC right — per seat, per use, per outcome? Does it scale with the value received, or punish the behavior you want?
4. What price range do the four PSM bounds imply for this segment — and is the recommendation inside it or asserted?
5. What does the price SIGNAL (₹199 says commodity; ₹19,999 says consulting) — does the signal match the positioning?
6. Where is the tier ladder's upgrade trigger, and what share of users will ever hit it? (An unhittable ladder is decoration.)

## How to apply
- Demand a value-anchor calculation: problem cost per month vs price per month, with the ratio stated.
- Weak answer smell: "competitive pricing at ₹X"; one price for segments with 10× different value; margins quoted without the arithmetic (price − unit cost shown).
- Does NOT apply: pure landscape questions with no monetization decision.

## Output contract
Revision must show price derivation from at least two independent anchors (WTP bounds, alternative cost, value share), justify the value metric, and stress the price at both segment extremes.
