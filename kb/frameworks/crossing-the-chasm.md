## Essence
Early adopters buy promise; the mainstream buys references and completeness — and a chasm sits between them. Crossing requires a NARROW beachhead segment you can dominate, the WHOLE product that segment needs (integrations, support, compliance — not just the core), and word-of-mouth density within that segment before expanding bowling-pin style.

## Interrogation set
1. What is the beachhead — one segment, nameable in a sentence, winnable to >50% share? "SMB e-commerce sellers in India" is a market; it is not a beachhead.
2. Is early traction from visionaries being extrapolated to pragmatists who buy on references, not demos?
3. What does the WHOLE product require for the beachhead (onboarding, integrations, compliance, local language/payment) — and what's missing from the draft's scope?
4. Where will the first 10 referenceable customers come from, and do they talk to each other? (Word-of-mouth needs segment density.)
5. What is the bowling-pin sequence — which segment follows the beachhead, and what carries over?
6. Does the adoption forecast implicitly assume chasm-free diffusion (smooth S-curve from day one)?

## How to apply
- Force a "we will dominate X before touching Y" sentence; refusal to narrow is the finding.
- Weak answer smell: TAM-wide GTM; channel lists instead of segment choice; "product-led growth" as a substitute for a beachhead.
- Does NOT apply: mature-category products selling to an established buying motion.

## Output contract
Revision must name one beachhead with domination logic, a whole-product gap list, a reference-density plan, and a staged expansion sequence.
