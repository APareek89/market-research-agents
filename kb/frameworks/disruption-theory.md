## Essence
Disruption is a specific mechanism, not a synonym for "new": an entrant takes root where incumbents are structurally UNMOTIVATED to fight — either overserved low-end customers or non-consumers — then improves upstream. A "better product for the incumbent's best customers" is sustaining innovation, and incumbents usually win those fights.

## Interrogation set
1. Is this thesis genuinely disruptive (low-end or new-market foothold) or sustaining (better product, same customers)? The draft's survival odds differ by an order of magnitude.
2. Where is the incumbent OVERSHOOTING — which customers are paying for performance they don't use? That's the entry seam; does the draft locate it?
3. Why would the incumbent rationally IGNORE this entry (margin dilution, channel conflict, too-small segment) — and for how long?
4. What is the improvement trajectory that carries the foothold upmarket — or is the foothold a permanent niche?
5. If the entrant thesis is "same customers, better features": what stops the incumbent from copying in one release cycle?
6. Who else is attacking the same foothold, and does being second still work?

## How to apply
- Demand the incumbent's P&L logic for not responding — "they're slow" is hope, not analysis.
- Weak answer smell: "disruptive" used as an adjective for impressive; entry aimed at the incumbent's most profitable segment.
- Does NOT apply: greenfield categories with no incumbent.

## Output contract
Revision must classify the move (sustaining vs low-end vs new-market), name the overshoot seam and the incumbent's rational-ignore window, and show the upmarket trajectory or admit the niche.
