## Essence
Assume the recommended plan was executed and it failed — completely, embarrassingly. Reason *backwards* from the certain failure to its most plausible causes. Prospective hindsight ("it HAS failed") measurably surfaces more and different risks than "what might go wrong?".

## Interrogation set
1. It is 18 months later and this recommendation failed badly. Write the honest post-mortem headline — what killed it?
2. Which single assumption, if wrong, unravels the whole thesis? Where does the draft even acknowledge it?
3. What would a smart skeptic bet against in this plan, and at what odds?
4. Which failure cause is INTERNAL (execution, cannibalization, org capacity) rather than market? Drafts almost always externalize failure.
5. What early-warning indicator would show the failure coming, and does the recommendation define it?
6. What is the cost of being wrong versus the cost of being 6 months late? Which error is recoverable?

## How to apply
- Demand at least 3 distinct failure causes with mechanism, not "competition might react".
- Weak answer smell: risks listed post-hoc with no probability, no indicator, no mitigation owner; a "risks" table that never touches the recommendation.
- Does NOT apply: purely descriptive market overviews with no recommended action.

## Output contract
Revision must carry a failure-modes section where each mode has: mechanism → leading indicator → mitigation or explicit acceptance. The recommendation must state its kill-switch condition.
