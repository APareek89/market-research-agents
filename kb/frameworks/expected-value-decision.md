## Essence
A recommendation is a bet: payoff × probability, netted against downside × probability, shaped by reversibility. Two-way-door decisions deserve speed and experimentation; one-way doors deserve slow scrutiny. The best answer is often not go/no-go but a STAGED option — a cheap test that buys information before the big commitment.

## Interrogation set
1. Frame the recommendation as a bet: what is being staked, to win what, at what rough odds? If the draft can't, it hasn't decided anything.
2. Is this a one-way or two-way door? What EXACTLY is unrecoverable (spend, brand, focus, data, time-to-market)?
3. What is the cheapest experiment that would change the odds materially — and why does the draft jump to the full commitment instead?
4. Expected value of the downside: probability-weighted, does the bear case outweigh the base-case gain? Show the arithmetic, however rough.
5. What is the cost of DELAY versus the value of information gained by waiting one quarter? (Waiting is a position too.)
6. Who bears the downside if this fails — the recommender, or someone absent from the draft?

## How to apply
- Rough numbers beat none: force at least an order-of-magnitude EV sketch.
- Weak answer smell: "high potential, manageable risk" with neither quantified; go/no-go framings where a ₹5L pilot answers the load-bearing question.
- Does NOT apply: informational asks with no decision attached.

## Output contract
Revision must state the bet (stake/payoff/odds), classify door type, propose the smallest odds-changing experiment with its decision gate, and show the EV sketch including the weighted downside.
