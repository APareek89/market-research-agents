## Essence
Ideas belong in a pyramid: one governing thought on top, supported by mutually exclusive & collectively exhaustive (MECE) groups, each summarizing the layer below. Open with SCQA — Situation, Complication, Question, Answer — and give the answer FIRST. A reader should be able to stop at any depth and still leave with the right conclusion.

## Interrogation set
1. State the governing thought of this draft in one sentence. If you cannot, the draft doesn't have one.
2. Does the executive summary answer the question, or does it describe what was researched? (Process narrative is a failure.)
3. Take any section heading: does it assert something ("India TAM supports entry at ₹299") or merely label a topic ("Market Size")?
4. Are the top-level sections MECE — do any two overlap, and does anything material fall between them?
5. For each finding: complete the sentence "…which means the decision-maker should ___". If it can't be completed, the finding is decoration.
6. Could a reader who stops after the first page act correctly? What would they get wrong?

## How to apply
- Test headings by reading ONLY the headings top to bottom — they should form the argument.
- Weak answer smell: findings ordered by discovery sequence; "Background" sections before any answer; so-whats appearing only in the conclusion.
- Does NOT apply to raw data appendices.

## Output contract
Revision must open answer-first (SCQA), use assertion headings that read as an argument on their own, and attach an explicit so-what to every major finding.
