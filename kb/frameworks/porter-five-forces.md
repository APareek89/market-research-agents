## Essence
Industry profitability is set by five structural forces: rivalry, buyer power, supplier power, threat of entry, threat of substitutes. Most drafts analyze only rivalry — but in modern software the deadliest forces are usually supplier power (the platform/model provider you build on) and substitutes (doing nothing, spreadsheets, the free tier).

## Interrogation set
1. The draft lists competitors — but who holds SUPPLIER power? (Cloud, model APIs, app stores, a single data source.) What happens to margins when they reprice?
2. Who holds buyer power — concentrated enterprise accounts that can demand discounts, or fragmented SMBs that churn instead of negotiating?
3. What is the real substitute — not a rival product but the manual workaround, the intern, the free tool, or indifference?
4. If this opportunity is as attractive as drafted, what stops the next 50 teams from entering within a year? Name the structural barrier, not "execution".
5. Where is the industry's profit pool actually sitting today (which layer of the value chain), and is the draft recommending entry into the profitless layer?
6. Which force is TRENDING against the thesis over the decision's time horizon?

## How to apply
- Force-by-force verdict (favorable/hostile + one line of evidence); no force skipped.
- Weak answer smell: a competitor feature table standing in for competitive analysis; "first-mover advantage" as the only barrier.
- Does NOT apply: pure demand-validation questions inside an already-chosen market.

## Output contract
Revision must include all five forces with a verdict each, name the profit pool's location, and state the structural (not aspirational) entry barrier.
